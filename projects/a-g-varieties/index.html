A&G Varieties Store

A product-focused storefront concept that explored catalogue discovery, management tools and a clean modern identity for a growing retail business.

The brief

The project explored how a small retail brand could move from informal product sharing to a more organised online storefront. Shoppers needed a clearer way to see what was available, while the business needed a foundation for managing products behind the scenes.

The visual direction also needed to feel more deliberate than a generic catalogue, giving the store room to build a recognisable identity around its range of products.

The solution

I designed the experience around large product imagery, simple browsing and a consistent card system. The management side was planned around creating and maintaining catalogue entries without mixing administrative controls into the shopper experience.

The result became an early full-stack learning project: part storefront, part product-management exercise and part visual-brand exploration.

  • Product-led homepage and catalogue browsing
  • Reusable product cards and detail patterns
  • Administrative product-management direction
  • Responsive layout for mobile shopping
  • Modern retail visual identity
  • Database-backed catalogue foundation

What it taught me

A&G Varieties helped connect frontend decisions to the data behind them. A product card is not only a visual element; it needs a reliable content model, sensible image treatment, readable pricing and predictable behaviour across a growing catalogue.

The project also reinforced why current portfolio work uses clearer status labels and verified links. The original live deployment is no longer available, so this page preserves the design and learning value without sending visitors to a broken demo.

The outcome

The archived build remains an important step in the GOJO.DEV timeline. It moved the work beyond a static marketing page and into questions about products, data and administration.

Those lessons now carry into newer projects with stronger information architecture, clearer technical boundaries and more deliberate deployment checks.
